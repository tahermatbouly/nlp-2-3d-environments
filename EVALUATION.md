# Floorplan Diffusion Evaluation Metrics

This document provides a detailed explanation of the evaluation metrics generated by the floorplan diffusion model during inference. The metrics are divided into four main categories: Floorplan-level metrics, Room-level (Top 1) metrics, Room-level (Best of K) metrics, and Ground Truth Reference.

## 1. Floorplan-level Metrics

These metrics evaluate the overall quality and validity of the entire generated floorplan (the arrangement of all rooms).

- **`csr_top1`**: Constraint Satisfaction Rate (CSR) of the highest-scored layout candidate. This is the primary metric for generation quality. It measures the percentage of floorplans where *all* core constraints are satisfied (no invalid polygons, no severe room overlaps, area requests met, and required room connections are adjacent). 
- **`csr_top1_strict`**: A stricter version of `csr_top1` that additionally requires zero spurious edges (i.e., rooms that should *not* be connected must not touch).
- **`csr_any_of_k`**: The percentage of floorplans where *at least one* of the `K` generated candidates satisfies all core constraints.
- **`csr_mean_over_k`**: The average constraint satisfaction rate across all `K` generated candidates.
- **`invalid_polygon_pct`**: The percentage of generated floorplans that contain at least one geometrically invalid room (e.g., a room with self-intersecting walls or zero area). Lower is better.
- **`room_invalid_pct`**: The average percentage of individual rooms within a floorplan that are geometrically invalid.
- **`overlap_viol_pct`**: The percentage of floorplans where room overlap exceeds the acceptable threshold. High overlap means rooms are occupying the same physical space. Lower is better.
- **`area_ok_pct`**: The percentage of floorplans where the generated area of *all* rooms matches the requested area from the conditioning graph within an acceptable tolerance.
- **`connectivity_acc`**: The percentage of requested edges (connections between rooms) that are successfully satisfied. A connection is satisfied if the two room polygons are touching or within the connectivity tolerance.
- **`spurious_edge_rate`**: The rate at which rooms that are *not* supposed to be connected end up touching each other in the generated layout. Lower is better.
- **`topology_consistent_pct`**: The percentage of floorplans where the exact requested topology is met (perfect connectivity recall AND zero spurious edges).
- **`geometric_validity_pct`**: The percentage of floorplans that are purely geometrically valid (zero invalid polygons and no severe overlaps), disregarding area and connectivity constraints.

## 2. Room-level Metrics (`room_top1` and `room_best_of_k`)

These metrics compare the individual generated rooms against the Ground Truth layout. Since many valid floorplans can be generated for a single graph, these metrics measure structural similarity rather than absolute validity.
`room_top1` calculates these for the highest-ranked candidate, while `room_best_of_k` calculates them for the candidate that most closely resembles the ground truth (highest IoU).

- **`area_err`**: The average relative absolute error between the generated room's area and the requested area.
- **`centroid_err`**: The average Euclidean distance between the generated room's center and the ground truth room's center.
- **`iou`**: Intersection over Union. Measures how much the generated room overlaps with the ground truth room (after aligning their centers). A higher IoU means the shape matches the ground truth better.
- **`coord_err`**: The average distance between the vertices of the generated polygon and the ground truth polygon (only calculated when the vertex counts match).
- **`vcount_acc`**: The accuracy of the model in predicting the exact same number of polygon vertices (corners) as the ground truth room.

## 3. Ground Truth Reference

These are the metrics calculated on the actual real-world floorplans in the dataset using the exact same evaluation script.

- They serve as the **upper bound** or "ceiling" for the model. For example, if the real floorplans have a `csr` of 89.4%, you cannot expect the generative model to easily exceed this score, as even the real data has slight imperfections or labeling inconsistencies (e.g., minor overlaps or slight gaps between rooms).
